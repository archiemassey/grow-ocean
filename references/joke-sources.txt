ORIGIN AND ATTRIBUTION
The pinned README describes KarlJoke as a free, open-source dad-joke API
inspired by https://github.com/15Dkatz/official_joke_api. It explains the
alternative service in terms of unavailable service / unaccepted pull
requests upstream. Its Contributing section welcomes submissions and gives
the jokes.json record structure. It lists Eklavya Chandra as the project
author; the explicit MIT notice names Eklavya as copyright holder.

The pinned 2021/2022 snapshots provide historical evidence of collections
predating the modern generative-AI joke boom. Most sources explicitly invite
community submissions; petershank/jokes describes itself simply as a
collection. These facts do not prove the individual original author of each joke.
No guarantee of independently verified human authorship, original
authorship by the repository maintainer, or clearance of every possible
third-party claim is made. The explicit repository licences, with no separate
restrictive data licence found, are the stated basis for reuse. A repository
licence cannot independently clear a contributor's third-party infringement.
The source-specific origin evidence and full notices are reproduced below.
No joke-article scraping was used.

CURATION
Output: js/data/curated-jokes.json
All 379 original KarlJoke setup/punchline pairs were read during initial
curation. For this expansion every included additional complete joke was
read individually, including both fields of setup/punchline records.
437 jokes were selected in total: KarlJoke 238, Official Joke API 12,
JokeAPI 63, Yes Interactive 91, Mike McBride 13, petershank 20.

Selection prioritised coherence, variety and clean broad adult humour.
Explicit, sexual, degrading, gory, violent, suicide, racist, religious or
political targeting, incomplete and weaker entries, exact duplicates and
manually identified near-duplicate variants were omitted. Secular holiday
references, mild bathroom humour, nonviolent fictional crime puns and
familiar fictional characters may occur; these are not children's-only jokes.

The setup and punchline strings were copied unchanged, not generated or
rewritten. Genuine one-liners retain their complete original text as prompt
without an invented answer. Upstream spelling, punctuation, whitespace and
occasional minor imperfections remain. For petershank's dated entries,
literal Q:/A: field labels and separate date annotations are omitted, not
joke wording. Its knock-knock dialogue uses the source's existing final
answer line; no artificial wording or punchline was added.

Every included entry identifies its original numeric ID, one-based array
position or source line (as explicitly labelled), with a pinned line citation.
KarlJoke has a repeated ID 189; neither ambiguous record was selected.
Official Joke API has no record IDs in the pinned data; its positions are
our stable references into that exact snapshot, not invented upstream IDs.
McBride one-liners are decoded original JavaScript string literals; source
line numbers count newline characters, not Unicode paragraph separators.

Categories (one best-fitting category per joke):
animals (86) - animal-centred jokes
food (66) - food, drink and cooking
everyday (137) - daily life, family, work, sports, books and situations
wordplay (80) - general puns without a stronger subject
knock-knock (6) - complete knock-knock format; format takes precedence
science-tech (62) - science, space, maths and technology

The historical sources contain few complete, accessible knock-knock jokes;
this category deliberately has fewer entries than the others rather than
adding weak material or generating replacements. The final JSON records
the exact selected total and per-category counts in provenance.

Validation checked preservation of the original 202 records, unique output
IDs, normalized setup, whole-joke and nonempty punchline
uniqueness (case, whitespace and punctuation ignored), nonempty coverage
of all six categories, and exact setup/punchline equality with the pinned
upstream records. Near-duplicate screening also involved manual reading;
mechanical normalization alone cannot establish semantic uniqueness.
Humour and suitability are subjective; this is editorial curation, not an
assurance that every reader will enjoy every joke.

RESEARCH FRONTIER / WHY THIS IS NOT 1000
The requested target was about 1000; this release contains 437, not 1000.
The six reviewed sources overlap heavily. Additional entries were rejected
for suitability, coherence, repetition or highly specialist material, rather
than treating a repository's advertised size as a usable-joke count.

kylecs/jokes-dataset-json: verified repository, README, all.json, format.py
and scrape.py tree, but no licence file or repository licence; excluded.
https://github.com/kylecs/jokes-dataset-json
rdegges/dad_jokes: the suggested repository returned GitHub HTTP 404.
Search-engine assertions that these are MIT collections were not trusted.

Large scraped aggregations such as taivop/joke-dataset and the Short Jokes
dataset were not used: a code licence or dataset-host metadata does not
establish rights to redistribute the scraped joke texts. Suggested joke
article collections were not scraped. Older Project Gutenberg conundrum
books were investigated but not included: many entries are archaic,
derogatory, obscure or riddles rather than suitable modern jokes, and the
site explicitly requires non-US users to check local copyright law.
Other inspected small collections largely duplicated the selected bank or
were overwhelmingly specialist programming jokes. We did not manufacture,
rewrite, clone or count weak variants merely to hit the numerical target.

This is the achieved research frontier, not a claim that no other suitable
licensed source exists. Reaching 1000 requires additional verified sources
or permission from their authors, followed by full review and deduplication.
